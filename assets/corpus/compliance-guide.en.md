# Compliance Guide

> Version: 2026.0, Compliance Office
> This guide applies to all employees, officers, directors, contractors and third
> parties acting on the company's behalf. It supplements, and does not replace,
> applicable laws and the company's Code of Business Conduct.

## 1. Purpose and Scope

1.1 The purpose of this guide is to help everyone act with integrity and in compliance with the law and company policy.

1.2 The guide covers all jurisdictions where the company operates.

1.3 If local law is more restrictive than this guide, local law applies.

1.4 If this guide is more restrictive than local law, this guide applies.

1.5 Compliance is every employee's responsibility, not just the Compliance Office's.

1.6 Managers are responsible for setting an example and for reporting suspected violations.

1.7 You must read this guide and complete the annual compliance training.

1.8 Questions about this guide should be directed to compliance@example.com.

1.9 Ignorance of the policy is not a defence in an investigation.

1.10 The Compliance Office may update this guide; updates are announced by email.

1.11 This guide does not create an employment contract or legal right for any third party.

1.12 References to "you" mean any person covered by this guide.

## 2. Code of Business Conduct

2.1 We act honestly, fairly and with integrity in all business dealings.

2.2 We comply with the laws and regulations of every jurisdiction in which we operate.

2.3 We do not discriminate or harass; we respect the dignity of all people.

2.4 We protect company assets and use them only for legitimate business purposes.

2.5 We avoid conflicts of interest and disclose them when they arise.

2.6 We compete fairly and never use unlawful means to obtain an advantage.

2.7 We protect confidential information and personal data entrusted to us.

2.8 We keep accurate books, records and business documents.

2.9 We cooperate fully with internal investigations and regulatory authorities.

2.10 We do not retaliate against anyone who raises a concern in good faith.

2.11 We represent the company responsibly in public and on social media.

2.12 We decline business that would require violating this Code.

2.13 We report suspected violations promptly through the channels in Section 12.

2.14 Business decisions are judged by their substance and their appearance.

2.15 A decision that would look wrong if published in the newspaper is probably wrong.

## 3. Gifts and Entertainment

3.1 Employees may not accept gifts valued over USD 50 from any business partner.

3.2 Gifts under USD 50 must still be reasonable, infrequent and not offered to influence a decision.

3.3 Cash, cash equivalents, gift cards and personal loans are prohibited in all circumstances.

3.4 Modest business meals are acceptable when they are infrequent and properly documented.

3.5 All entertainment expenses over USD 100 require prior approval from the Compliance Officer.

3.6 No gift or entertainment may be offered or accepted during an active procurement or contract negotiation.

3.7 We never offer gifts or entertainment to government officials without prior legal review.

3.8 We do not accept gifts from a party whose business we can influence or are evaluating.

3.9 Vendors may not be coerced or pressured into providing gifts or perks.

3.10 Gift registers are maintained by the Compliance Office for gifts over USD 50.

3.11 Undisclosed acceptance of repeated small gifts may still constitute a conflict of interest.

3.12 Employees must decline, return, or report any gift that exceeds the threshold.

3.13 Conference registration, travel and lodging paid by a third party require pre-approval.

3.14 Sponsorship of company events by vendors is allowed only under a written agreement.

3.15 Seasonal tokens of appreciation (e.g. holiday fruit baskets) are evaluated case by case.

3.16 The same rules apply to gifts given to a spouse or family member on your behalf.

3.17 Do not offer gifts contingent on a future business decision.

3.18 Records of offered and received gifts are retained for audit purposes.

## 4. Conflicts of Interest

4.1 A conflict of interest exists when personal interests could influence company decisions.

4.2 Employees must disclose any personal financial interest in a vendor or customer.

4.3 Disclosures are made through the annual conflict-of-interest declaration.

4.4 Procurement staff must recuse themselves from decisions involving immediate family members.

4.5 Employees may not use their position to obtain personal advantages from customers or vendors.

4.6 Outside employment or directorships must be declared and approved in advance.

4.7 Owning a material stake in a competitor, customer or supplier must be disclosed.

4.8 Hiring a family member to work on the same team requires HR approval.

4.9 Managing a family member directly or indirectly is prohibited without approval.

4.10 Employees may not award business to a company they control or benefit from.

4.11 Personal use of company assets, staff or data is not permitted.

4.12 Investment decisions must not be based on confidential company information.

4.13 A perceived conflict must be managed even when no actual conflict exists.

4.14 Managers who learn of an undisclosed conflict must escalate to the Compliance Office.

4.15 Failure to disclose a material conflict is a disciplinary matter.

4.16 The Compliance Office records and manages all declared conflicts.

## 5. Anti-Bribery and Corruption

5.1 We prohibit bribery in all forms, whether direct or indirect.

5.2 We never offer, promise, give or accept anything of value to improperly influence a decision.

5.3 The prohibition applies to government officials, customers and private parties alike.

5.4 Facilitation payments are prohibited unless required by applicable law.

5.5 Third parties acting on our behalf must not make improper payments either.

5.6 All payments to third parties must be supported by legitimate documentation.

5.7 Kickbacks and undisclosed commissions are prohibited.

5.8 We perform due diligence on agents and intermediaries before engagement.

5.9 Our anti-bribery controls follow internationally recognised frameworks.

5.10 Suspected bribery must be reported immediately to the Compliance Office.

5.11 No employee is penalised for refusing to participate in improper conduct.

5.12 We do not retaliate against employees who decline or report improper demands.

5.13 Political contributions must never be used as a vehicle for bribery.

5.14 Charitable donations must be genuine and cannot be tied to business favours.

5.15 Expense reports are reviewed to detect improper payments.

5.16 Our audit function samples high-risk transactions for corruption indicators.

## 6. Fraud Prevention

6.1 We do not tolerate fraud, theft, or misappropriation of company assets.

6.2 False claims in expense reports or timesheets constitute fraud.

6.3 Employees must not create, alter, or destroy records to misrepresent facts.

6.4 Accounts must reflect transactions accurately and in the correct period.

6.5 Segregation of duties protects both the company and the employee.

6.6 Approvers must verify the substance of what they approve.

6.7 Billing errors and overpayments must be corrected promptly.

6.8 Vendor master data changes require independent approval.

6.9 Automated controls flag duplicate invoices and anomalous spending.

6.10 Fraud suspicions are reported confidentially through the whistleblowing channel.

6.11 We cooperate with auditors and investigators on all fraud matters.

6.12 Unreported fraud risks are escalated by management to the Audit Committee.

6.13 Data manipulation to hit targets is treated as misconduct.

6.14 We do not knowingly process forged or fraudulent documents.

## 7. Data Privacy and Protection

7.1 Customer personal data must be handled in line with the internal Data Privacy Policy.

7.2 Never share customer data with third parties without a signed data-processing agreement.

7.3 Collect only the personal data needed for a stated business purpose.

7.4 Inform data subjects about how their data is used, and honour their rights.

7.5 Store personal data only as long as required and delete it securely afterwards.

7.6 Access to personal data is on a need-to-know basis and logged.

7.7 We do not process special categories of data (e.g. health, ethnicity) without legal basis.

7.8 International data transfers follow approved transfer mechanisms.

7.9 Personal data breaches are reported to the Privacy Officer without undue delay.

7.10 Employees may not use customer data for personal purposes.

7.11 Pseudonymisation is used where it reduces risk without harming business purpose.

7.12 Privacy impact assessments are performed for high-risk processing.

7.13 Automated decisions that significantly affect individuals are reviewed for fairness.

7.14 Third-party processors must provide equivalent privacy safeguards.

7.15 We respect data subject access, correction and deletion requests within legal timeframes.

7.16 Marketing communications require consent where required by law.

7.17 We do not sell personal data.

## 8. Insider Trading and Securities Compliance

8.1 Employees with access to material non-public information may not trade the company's securities during restricted windows.

8.2 "Material" information is anything a reasonable investor would consider important.

8.3 "Non-public" information is information not generally available to the market.

8.4 You must report all trades in company securities to the Compliance Office.

8.5 Trading during the open window still requires pre-clearance for insiders.

8.6 You may not trade on the basis of material non-public information, however obtained.

8.7 You may not tip others, including family and friends, with material non-public information.

8.8 Short-term trading in company securities by insiders is discouraged.

8.9 Hedging or pledging company securities requires pre-approval.

8.10 We do not trade during blackout periods, usually around financial reporting dates.

8.11 If you are unsure whether information is material, assume it is and ask Compliance.

8.12 Dealings in other companies' securities based on our confidential relationships are also restricted.

8.13 Employees who receive a personal trading inquiry from a regulator must notify Compliance.

8.14 Trading policies apply equally to immediate family members and controlled accounts.

## 9. Anti-Money Laundering (AML)

9.1 We comply with applicable anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing laws.

9.2 We conduct customer due diligence appropriate to the risk profile.

9.3 We verify the identity of counterparties before entering into significant transactions.

9.4 We do not accept cash payments above legally permitted thresholds.

9.5 We screen counterparties against sanctions and enforcement lists.

9.6 We monitor transactions for unusual or suspicious patterns.

9.7 Suspicious activity is reported to the MLRO without tipping off the customer.

9.8 We keep records required by AML regulations for the prescribed retention period.

9.9 We do not structure transactions to avoid reporting thresholds.

9.10 Politically exposed persons (PEPs) are subject to enhanced due diligence.

9.11 Anonymous or opaque beneficial ownership is treated as a red flag.

9.12 We refuse to facilitate transactions we cannot properly verify.

9.13 Employees must not knowingly handle the proceeds of crime.

9.14 AML training is mandatory for client-facing roles.

## 10. Sanctions and Export Controls

10.1 We comply with economic sanctions administered by applicable authorities.

10.2 We do not do business with sanctioned persons, entities or jurisdictions.

10.3 Export of controlled technology or goods requires the appropriate authorisation.

10.4 We screen counterparties, end-users and end-uses before engaging.

10.5 We do not facilitate a third party's evasion of sanctions.

10.6 Dual-use and military goods are handled only with legal clearance.

10.7 Software downloads and transfers to sanctioned jurisdictions are restricted.

10.8 Sanctions screening results are escalated to the Compliance Office for review.

10.9 We maintain audit trails of sanctioned-party screening.

10.10 Uncertainties about sanctions exposure must be escalated, not assumed away.

## 11. Records Management

11.1 Business records are created and retained according to the records retention schedule.

11.2 Records are classified by sensitivity and handled accordingly.

11.3 We do not destroy records that are subject to a legal hold.

11.4 Litigation holds suspend normal deletion rules and are binding on all staff.

11.5 Emails, chat logs and shared documents are business records.

11.6 Personal files must not be mixed with business records on company systems.

11.7 Records are stored in approved repositories, not personal devices.

11.8 Retention periods follow regulatory and tax requirements.

11.9 Destruction of records is performed securely and logged.

11.10 If you are unsure about a hold, ask Legal before deleting anything.

11.11 Duplicate and obsolete records are disposed of promptly.

11.12 Backups are managed centrally and are not user-addressable.

## 12. Whistleblowing and Reporting Concerns

12.1 We encourage employees to raise concerns in good faith without fear of retaliation.

12.2 Concerns can be reported to your manager, HR, or the Compliance Office.

12.3 An anonymous reporting hotline is available 24/7.

12.4 Reports may be made in any language and are taken seriously.

12.5 The identity of reporters is protected to the extent permitted by law.

12.6 We do not tolerate retaliation against good-faith reporters.

12.7 Retaliation itself is a serious disciplinary offence.

12.8 Reports are investigated promptly, fairly and confidentially.

12.9 Investigators document findings and actions taken.

12.10 The Board Audit Committee receives periodic summaries of reports.

12.11 Anonymous reporters are offered secure channels to receive follow-up.

12.12 Knowingly making a false report is treated as misconduct.

12.13 Concerns about accounting or audit matters follow the special escalation path.

## 51. Frequently Asked Questions

51.1 Q: What do I do if I receive a gift over USD 50? A: Decline, return, or report it to Compliance and record it in the gift register.

51.2 Q: Can I accept a meal from a vendor? A: Modest, infrequent business meals are acceptable if documented and not tied to a decision.

51.3 Q: What is a facilitation payment? A: A small payment to speed up a routine action; it is prohibited unless required by law.

51.4 Q: Who do I tell if I suspect bribery? A: Report immediately to Compliance via email or the anonymous hotline.

51.5 Q: Can I trade company stock during blackout? A: No; insiders may trade only in open windows with pre-clearance.

51.6 Q: When is a conflict of interest a problem? A: When personal interests could influence or appear to influence company decisions.

51.7 Q: How do I declare a conflict? A: Use the annual conflict-of-interest declaration or report to Compliance when it arises.

51.8 Q: Can I share customer data with a partner? A: Only with a signed data-processing agreement and a legitimate business need.

51.9 Q: What happens if I make a mistake? A: Report it early; early, honest reporting is treated more favourably than concealment.

51.10 Q: Am I protected if I report a concern? A: Yes; we prohibit retaliation against good-faith reporters.

51.11 Q: Can I send a gift to a government official? A: No, without prior legal review and approval.

51.12 Q: How do I request a compliance opinion? A: Email compliance@example.com with the facts; you will receive a written response.

## 52. Glossary of Terms

52.12 Inside Information: Material non-public information about a company.

52.13 Insider: A person with access to inside information.

52.14 Kickback: An improper payment in return for business advantage.

52.15 MLRO: Money Laundering Reporting Officer, the designated AML reporting role.

52.17 PEP: Politically Exposed Person, subject to enhanced due diligence.

52.18 Privacy Impact Assessment: A review of the privacy risks of processing.
